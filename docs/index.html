Nobody has to get their phone out.

Three shortcuts that check your kids in and out of Brightwheel by themselves, while you are still parking.

Will this work for you?

  • An iPhone running iOS 27 Not iOS 26 or anything older, where these stop at the very first step. Settings, then General, then About, then Software Version.
  • A Brightwheel account you sign into with an email and password The same one you use in the app.
  • Your own four-digit check-in code The one you tap on the school's iPad. It is what tells Brightwheel the check-in came from you.

Putting it on your phone

  1. Download the three shortcuts

    Get the shortcuts

    Tap each of the three files ending in .shortcut, and tap Download when your phone asks.

  2. Open them from the Files app

    Nothing appears to happen when you tap Download. The files are in the Files app, under Downloads. Tap each one there and Shortcuts offers to add it.

    The order does not matter, but all three have to be on the phone before anything will run on its own.

  3. Answer the three questions

    Only Brightwheel Attendance asks anything: your email, your password, and your four-digit code. It asks them one at a time.

    On the last question, tap Skip Setup, not Add Shortcut.

    Add Shortcut does nothing at all on iOS 27 — no error, no shortcut. Skip Setup saves everything you typed. This is a bug in iOS, not in anything you did.

    Check the first letter of your password.

    The box capitalizes it for you, so hunter2 is saved as Hunter2 and signing in later fails with a password that looks right.

  4. Run it once yourself, at school

    Open Brightwheel Check In and run it by hand the first time. It signs you in, which needs a code sent to you, and a shortcut running on its own cannot type that in.

    Do this at the school. It may ask to scan the check-in code taped up by the door.

  5. Tell it where school is

    Add an arrival trigger to Brightwheel Check In and another to Brightwheel Check Out. Each one shows you where, the first time you run it.

    Use Arrive for both, on two time ranges that do not overlap — morning for dropping off, afternoon for picking up. Set Location Services for Shortcuts to Always, or the trigger never fires.

What it knows about you

Your password never leaves your phone
It is stored inside the shortcut, on your device, and sent only to Brightwheel when it signs in.
The download holds nothing about your family
Everyone downloads the same file. It asks Brightwheel who your children are every time it runs, so a new sibling or a room change needs nothing from you.
Apple signed the file
Which is why your iPhone opens it without warning you about where it came from.
It stops rather than guessing
If it cannot tell who your children are, or one of them turns up in two rooms, it sends nothing and says so. A half-finished check-in is worse than none, because you would believe it worked.
